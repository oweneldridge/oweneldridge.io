I was technical lead for payments at Indigo Tech Group, a Mindbody company.
Indigo has since been renamed Kite Technologies and is now a registered
payment facilitator.
My work sat on the side of it where moving money stops being an
engineering problem and starts being a regulatory one.

## What a payment facilitator takes on

Becoming a payment facilitator means taking on obligations a bank would
otherwise hold. Before a merchant can process a payment, somebody has to
establish that the business is real, that the people behind it are who they
claim, and that none of them appear on a sanctions list. That is beneficial
ownership verification and screening, and most of it is data work: identity
evidence arrives incomplete, entities change shape, and the answer has to
hold up months later when somebody asks how you reached it.

<Note>

The public rules behind it: FinCEN's [customer due diligence rule](https://www.fincen.gov/resources/statutes-and-regulations/cdd-final-rule) and OFAC's [sanctions lists](https://ofac.treasury.gov/specially-designated-nationals-and-blocked-persons-list-sdn-human-readable-lists).

</Note>

The rest of what I worked on had the same character. Card-present
processing from terminal communication through to the stored transaction,
integrating a processor's cloud API. Webhook subscription management on
Lambda and DynamoDB, so external systems learned about payment events in
close to real time. A payment component embedded in the core application
for card and bank account entry. PCI compliance was a standing constraint
on every one of those decisions from the start.

<Note>

The card industry's security standard: [PCI DSS](https://www.pcisecuritystandards.org/standards/pci-dss/).

</Note>

## What carried over to claims

Payments taught me the habit I now use on pharmacy claims. The failures
that matter are values that look completely plausible and are wrong, in
systems where nobody notices until money or a regulator does. A funds
transfer that reconciles to the wrong fee, an entity that passes a check it
should have failed, a transaction stored in a shape that reads fine until
someone asks a question a year later.

So I write tests around the monetary edge cases, and I would rather build a
verification step than trust a report.
